The result of September's Italian Grand Prix is not settled yet. The FIA International Court of Appeal will hear Audi's appeal against Yuki Tsunoda's 10th place at Monza on 25 November at the FIA's Paris headquarters, [Motorsport Week reports](https://www.motorsportweek.com/2026/11/13/yuki-tsunoda-f1-audi/). [GPblog](https://www.gpblog.com/en/news/f1-today-fia-bans-f1-journalist-after-leclerc-jibe-as-tsunoda-faces-key-hearing) says the hearing will take place behind closed doors at 09:00 local time, with a verdict expected as soon as possible afterwards.

## What happened at Monza

Tsunoda was driving for Racing Bulls as a stand-in for Liam Lawson, who had moved up to Red Bull to replace the injured Isack Hadjar, according to Motorsport Week. At the restart after a red flag, Tsunoda lined up in the wrong grid slot, tried to correct it and was left at an angle, and race director Rui Marques ordered an extra formation lap.

Audi argued that this triggered Article B5.9.4 of the Sporting Regulations, which says a driver who causes an extra formation lap must start from the end of the pit lane. The stewards disagreed. In their decision, quoted by Motorsport Week, they found that the extra lap "resulted from the Race Director's assessment of the situation", rather than being directly caused by Tsunoda.

## What is at stake

Tsunoda finished 10th and kept the point, the only one he scored in his three rounds with Racing Bulls this season. Gabriel Bortoleto was 11th for Audi. If the appeal succeeds, GPblog says, Bortoleto could inherit the final points-paying place.

Analysis: one point rarely changes much on its own, but in a tight midfield fight it can shift constructors' positions and the prize money attached to them, which helps explain why Audi has taken the case to the FIA's top tribunal two months after the race.

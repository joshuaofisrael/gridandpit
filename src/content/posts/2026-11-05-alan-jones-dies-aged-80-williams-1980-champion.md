Alan Jones, the Australian who won the 1980 Formula 1 World Championship with Williams, has died at the age of 80 after a short illness. Formula 1, Williams Racing, BBC Sport and Motorsport Australia all reported the news on 5 November 2026.

[Formula 1 wrote on X](https://x.com/F1/status/2118259872235929643): "We are very saddened to hear of the passing of World Champion Alan Jones. 'Jonesy' made his first F1 entrance at the 1975 Spanish Grand Prix with the Hesketh squad before going on to famously claim the title in 1980 alongside Frank Williams at his eponymous team." The championship's longer tribute is on [Formula1.com](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/f1-champion-alan-jones-passes-away-aged-80.47VhlGhEpLtaMwCOlLAB3Y).

## Family statement

A statement released on behalf of his wife, Amanda Butler-Davis, by [Motorsport Australia](https://motorsport.org.au/media/news/detail/2026/11/05/statement-on-behalf-of-amanda-butler-davis/) said: "We are extremely saddened to announce the passing of our beloved Alan, who passed away on 5 November following a short illness." She thanked those who had supported him and said he left an enduring mark on the sport nationally and globally. [BBC Sport](https://www.bbc.com/sport/formula1/articles/cmd9345vdy8do) quotes the same statement and notes that Jones is survived by Amanda and his children Christian, Amber, Zara, Jack, Camilla and Emma.

## Career in brief

Born in Melbourne on 2 November 1946, Jones made his Formula 1 debut at the 1975 Spanish Grand Prix with Hesketh, Formula 1's obituary records. He also raced for Surtees, Hill, Shadow—with whom he took his first win at the 1977 Austrian Grand Prix—and later Beatrice Lola. Eleven of his 12 Grand Prix victories came with Williams.

Jones took Williams' first podium at Watkins Glen in 1978 and the team's first pole at Silverstone in 1979, then won five races from 14 in 1980 to become Australia's second world champion after Sir Jack Brabham. Technical chief Sir Patrick Head later recalled him as "the greatest fun, a terrific driver" whose arrival sharpened the team's focus, Formula 1 reports.

He finished third in the 1981 drivers' standings, won in Las Vegas that year for his final victory, retired at the end of the season, made a brief Arrows appearance in 1983 and returned full-time with Haas in 1985–86. After leaving Formula 1 he raced in other categories into the early 2000s and became a familiar broadcaster in Australia, BBC Sport notes. That second career kept him in front of Australian fans long after his last Grand Prix, which is why tributes on 5 November came as much from home motorsport institutions as from the current Formula 1 paddock.

BBC Sport also records that Jones remains the most recent Australian to win the Australian Grand Prix, taking the 1980 race at Calder Park when it was not yet a world championship round—an extra line of national sporting history alongside his world title.

## Tributes from the paddock

Formula 1 president and CEO Stefano Domenicali called Jones "a true great of our sport and an extraordinary racer" and highlighted his role in bringing Williams its first drivers' and constructors' titles in 1980. [Williams Racing](https://www.williamsf1.com/articles/01a04bb2-e7a1-443b-a29f-7b5b19dca939/alan-jones-1946-2026) said everyone at the team was deeply saddened, describing him as instrumental in making Williams what it is today and as a "true Australian icon and a Williams legend."

Jones turned 80 only days before his death. The tributes emphasise character as well as results: a fierce racer who helped turn Williams into a championship force and who remained closely identified with Australian motorsport long after his last Grand Prix.

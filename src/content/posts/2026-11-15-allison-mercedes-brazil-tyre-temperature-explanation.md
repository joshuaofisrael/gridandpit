Mercedes has now given a fuller account of why Kimi Antonelli and George Russell both thought their cars were broken during the Sao Paulo Grand Prix. Technical director James Allison told the team's Nu Silver Arrows Radio Show that the problem was tyre temperature, and that the team's drivers were far from alone, [PlanetF1 reports](https://www.planetf1.com/news/mercedes-reveals-brazilian-gp-investigation-findings).

## Puddles and cold slicks

Antonelli was leading when he switched from intermediates to slicks and then lost the rear under braking at Turn 1 on lap 20. He later finished fifth, behind Russell, who had spun at the pit exit.

Allison said many drivers in the pit lane reported the same trouble, adding that Max Verstappen "was particularly voluble on the topic". He explained that even though the tyres leave the garage in blankets, running through standing water strips the surface temperature, and drivers can then spend many laps trying to get heat back into them. In Antonelli's case, flat-spotting the tyres in the spin made recovery harder still, though Allison said his pace matched Lando Norris's once the car was stable.

When the rain returned late on, Allison said, both drivers were a little too aggressive on the throttle on their second set of intermediates while the track was still nearly dry, which damaged the rear tyres early in the stint.

## Lord's first explanation

Allison's comments follow deputy team principal Bradley Lord's debrief earlier in the week. Lord said the W17 was very oversteery and that it looked "just below the tyre temperature window", moving in and out of it in the damp, [Motorsport.com reported](https://www.motorsport.com/f1/news/mercedes-explains-unusual-car-behaviour-behind-kimi-antonellis-costly-brazil-spin/10873891/). He also noted that Mercedes had been the fastest car on track in parts of the race.

Analysis: Mercedes had already won the constructors' title in Brazil, so this was about understanding rather than damage control. With Las Vegas next and cool night conditions likely, getting heat into the tyres will matter again.

Update, 21 November 2026: this page was checked again against Article A2.2 of the [FIA 2026 Formula 1 Regulations, Section A, Issue 3](https://www.fia.com/system/files/documents/fia_2026_f1_regulations_-_section_a_general_provisions_-_iss_03_-_2026-06-25.pdf), the PDF dated 25 June 2026. The full distance scale was already right. The shortened race scale, the Sprint cutoff and the line about a fastest lap point were not, and they are rewritten below. Season totals built from race results are on the [records page](/records/).

A Grand Prix pays the top 10. A Sprint pays the top 8. Those points decide the drivers' title and the constructors' title. Both sit in Article A2.2 of that PDF.

## Grand Prix points

Article A2.2.1 pays a full distance race, one the leader has completed at 75 percent or more of the scheduled distance, like this:

- 1st: 25 points
- 2nd: 18
- 3rd: 15
- 4th: 12
- 5th: 10
- 6th: 8
- 7th: 6
- 8th: 4
- 9th: 2
- 10th: 1

Eleventh and lower score nothing. The same article has no extra point for the fastest lap. Older seasons are not retold here, because those books were not opened on 21 November 2026.

No points are paid at all unless the leader has completed two full laps in a row with no Safety Car and no Virtual Safety Car. That bar comes before the table.

## Shortened races

If the race ends early, the same article switches column by how far the leader got. These are the scales in the PDF, not a single half points rule.

| Place | Two laps, under 25 percent | 25 to under 50 percent | 50 to under 75 percent | 75 percent or more |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | 6 | 13 | 19 | 25 |
| 2nd | 4 | 10 | 14 | 18 |
| 3rd | 3 | 8 | 12 | 15 |
| 4th | 2 | 6 | 10 | 12 |
| 5th | 1 | 5 | 8 | 10 |
| 6th | none | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| 7th | none | 3 | 4 | 6 |
| 8th | none | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| 9th | none | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 10th | none | none | 1 | 1 |

## Sprint points

Article A2.2.2 pays a Sprint only if the leader has completed at least half of the scheduled Sprint distance, and only after the same two lap bar. The PDF does not set one Sprint length. It leaves that to the timetable for the weekend. At half distance or more, the scale is 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 for eighth. Under half distance, the article pays nothing.

A Sprint win is 8 points, the same as sixth in a full Grand Prix, and less than fourth, which is 12.

## The constructors' title

Article A2.1.4 gives the constructors' title to the team with the most points, counting both cars. Mercedes took that title in Sao Paulo, in the [constructors' title story](/articles/2026-11-09-mercedes-clinches-constructors-title-sao-paulo/).

If two cars finish the race tied for a place, Article A2.2.3 adds the points for those places and splits them evenly.

## Ties on the season table

If two drivers or two teams finish the season level, Article A2.1.4 gives the higher place to the one with more race wins, then more second places, then more third places, and so on. If that still fails, the same test moves to qualifying. The [tiebreak explainer](/articles/2026-10-27-f1-tiebreak-rules-explained/) walks through the Mexico City case. Antonelli's age on the title list is in [youngest F1 world champions](/articles/2026-10-28-youngest-f1-champions-antonelli/).

## A sum from the table

Article A2.1.2 caps the championship at 24 competitions. Twenty four wins at 25 points would be 600, before any Sprint. That is a ceiling in the rule, not a count of the 2026 calendar. Twenty second places are 360 points. Fourteen wins are 350. The second places would be ahead, on those sums alone.

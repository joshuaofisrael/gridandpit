Las Vegas qualifying was less about the fastest car and more about who was behind whom. "Everyone was looking for a tow in Q3 and nobody wanted to be at the front of the pack," George Russell said, in a quote [Mercedes posted on X](https://x.com/MercedesAMGF1/status/2124054170843979781). "Getting it right was very high-risk, though, as you had to leave it incredibly late to get the maximum benefit."

## Why the tow mattered so much

The Strip is a long straight, and sitting in another car's slipstream there is worth a lot of time. In Q1, McLaren and Ferrari both tried to choreograph slipstreaming between their drivers down The Strip, according to the [Formula1.com report](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/antonelli-quickest-in-las-vegas-gp-qualifying-as-verstappen-inherits-pole-position.6R7udh8iYMHunSOGI6zStK). In Q3, Mercedes, Alpine and Racing Bulls drivers "jostled for position on their out laps", and Kimi Antonelli only just started his final lap in time.

It paid off for him: he went fastest after starting that lap a second behind the car in front. Russell, who had complained over the radio that he needed better track position after an early run without a tow, jumped to third. He starts second because of Antonelli's five-place penalty.

## Ferrari's bad night

At Ferrari it went wrong a session earlier. "A difficult Quali in Las Vegas," the team [posted](https://x.com/ScuderiaFerrari/status/2124050183461724595), quoting Charles Leclerc: "We will give it our all and hopefully bring home some solid points in the race."

Leclerc had been best of the rest in Q1, but he ran deep on a quick lap in Q2 and missed the cut by just over a tenth, Formula1.com says. Lewis Hamilton finished half a second from Q3 after running off the track at Turn 1, reported a lack of grip and struggled to find a tow. They were 11th and 12th, and both should move up with penalties to drivers ahead of them.

## Tonight's race

The tow will matter again in the race, on a cold track with little dry running. Mercedes have Russell on the front row next to polesitter Max Verstappen, while Ferrari start from the middle of the pack and need a clean first lap.

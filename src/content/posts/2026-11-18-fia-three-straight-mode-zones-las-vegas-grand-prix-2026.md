The FIA has set three Straight Mode zones for this weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix, [PlanetF1 reports](https://www.planetf1.com/news/fia-las-vegas-grand-prix-2026-active-aero). Straight Mode is the low-drag setting of the 2026 cars' active aerodynamics: when it is used, the front and rear wings move to a lower-drag position for more speed. Drivers can use it only in zones the FIA chooses for each track.

## Where the zones are

- Zone 1 starts after Turn 4 and ends before Turn 5.
- Zone 2, the longest, starts after Turn 12, takes in the Turn 13 kink along the 1.9km straight and ends just before Turn 14.
- Zone 3 starts after Turn 16 and runs to Turn 17.

Overtake Mode detection is just after Turn 16. Overtake Mode gives a chasing car an extra 0.5MJ of energy if it is within one second of the car ahead at the detection point.

## How Vegas compares

The number of zones has varied a lot this season. PlanetF1 counts five at the season-opening Australian Grand Prix, the most of the year, and none at Monaco because of its tight layout. Las Vegas is also a street circuit, but its long straights leave room for three. The 6.2km, 17-turn track runs past landmarks including Caesars Palace, the Bellagio and the Venetian.

## Why it matters

With a 1.9km straight in Zone 2 and Overtake Mode detection placed just before the final zone, the layout gives a following driver two chances to attack in the last part of the lap. That matters for anyone starting out of position this weekend, including Oscar Piastri, who is set for a 10-place grid penalty.

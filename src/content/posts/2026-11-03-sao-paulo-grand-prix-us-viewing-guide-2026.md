[Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-time-is-the-formula-1-2026-sao-paulo-grand-prix-and-how-can-i-watch-it.6hpbTPt6gucIHBH8QztPjb) published its November 3 guide to the São Paulo Grand Prix, scheduled for November 6–8 at Interlagos. Friday practice is listed for 12:30 p.m. and 4 p.m. local time; Saturday’s final practice begins at 11:30 a.m., followed by qualifying at 3 p.m. The race starts Sunday at 2 p.m. São Paulo time, which is noon Eastern and 9 a.m. Pacific in the United States. The circuit is 4.309 kilometers long and the race is scheduled for 71 laps.

F1’s official preview notes a chance of rain across the weekend, particularly around Saturday, although forecasts can change. Interlagos combines elevation changes, a long straight and notable overtaking opportunities into Turn 1. U.S. viewers can follow coverage through the official F1 streaming options where available, while checking local television listings for their chosen provider.

## The U.S. timetable

Formula 1 confirmed the November 6–8 schedule on Tuesday. Friday's first practice starts at 12:30 p.m. São Paulo time, followed by a second session at 4 p.m. Saturday's final practice begins at 11:30 a.m. and qualifying at 3 p.m. The 71-lap Grand Prix starts Sunday at 2 p.m. local time. São Paulo is two hours ahead of U.S. Eastern Standard Time in November, making the Sunday start noon Eastern, 11 a.m. Central, 10 a.m. Mountain and 9 a.m. Pacific.

For U.S. viewers, Friday's sessions therefore begin at 10:30 a.m. and 2 p.m. Eastern; Saturday practice starts at 9:30 a.m. Eastern and qualifying at 1 p.m. Eastern. These conversions reflect the November time difference and should not be confused with U.S. daylight-saving schedules used earlier in the season.

## Where the cars will race

The event takes place at the Autódromo José Carlos Pace, widely known as Interlagos. Formula 1 says the current configuration has been used since 1990 and that the circuit has appeared on the calendar every year since then except 2020, when the COVID-19 pandemic interrupted the season. Its long main straight and overtaking opportunity into Turn 1 make the opening lap and restart sequences particularly important.

The official preview warned of possible heavy rain around Friday morning, thunderstorms on Saturday and lighter rain risk for Sunday. Those were forecasts published November 3, not confirmed race conditions. Fans should check updates closer to each session, because changes in rain intensity can influence tire selection and the amount of meaningful dry-weather preparation available.

## Championship context

Kimi Antonelli arrives as the youngest world champion in Formula 1 history after finishing third in Mexico City. Formula 1 credited him with nine victories this season. Mercedes also has an opportunity to clinch the constructors' championship in Brazil, though Ferrari and Red Bull remain competitive. Max Verstappen won the preceding race in Mexico, beating Lewis Hamilton by 0.799 seconds, so there is no reason to assume the new champion will automatically win at Interlagos.

## How to follow the action

Formula 1 lists F1 TV Pro as offering live sessions in eligible territories, including onboard camera feeds, while F1 TV Premium adds features such as 4K HDR and multiview on supported devices. Availability varies by country and subscription, so U.S. fans should check the current service and local broadcaster listings before purchasing. Official live timing is also available through F1's website and app. With the drivers' title settled but the constructors' championship still in play, the Brazil weekend offers a concrete competitive storyline as well as an early-Sunday viewing window for West Coast fans.

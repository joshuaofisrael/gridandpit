The FIA is considering a change to Formula 1's high-speed electrical deployment rules for 2027, [Grandprix.com reports](https://www.grandprix.com/news/fia-considers-deployment-rule-change-after-hamilton-criticism.html), citing The Race. The move follows Lewis Hamilton's complaint that the current system is hurting overtaking.

## What is being looked at

According to Grandprix.com, the subject will go to the teams through Formula 1's technical and sporting advisory committees. No change has been agreed, and the current rules stay in place for the rest of 2026.

The review is expected to focus on how electrical power is reduced on the straights, not on the basic design of the 2026 power units. That would let the FIA adjust the racing without asking engine manufacturers to start again.

## What clipping is

The 2026 power units deliver much more electrical power than the previous generation, but that help is cut back progressively at high speed. Once the battery contribution falls away, the car stops accelerating as strongly even though the combustion engine could take it faster. Drivers call this clipping.

## What Hamilton said

Hamilton raised it during the Mexico City weekend. "It's the silliest thing when you are behind and you have a little bit more power in the slipstream," the Ferrari driver said, as quoted by Grandprix.com. "You should be able to go past that to have a chance to overtake. The engine can go faster. It's just they have these silly clips." He added: "It doesn't provide great racing."

His point is that a following car can gain in the slipstream, only for the power cut to remove the advantage before the pass is done.

## Why Las Vegas matters

This weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix has long full-throttle sections, including the run down the Strip, and heavy braking zones. If cars close up but cannot finish the move, it will be another live example for the rule makers.

A proposal to let Formula 1's smaller teams spend more under the cost cap will be discussed at the F1 Commission on Monday. [The Race](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/aduo-for-teams-to-be-discussed-by-f1/) reports that Williams team principal James Vowles' plan has been added to the agenda under the name "ADUO for teams".

## What Vowles wants

Vowles argues the cost cap has made every team financially viable but has also locked in the advantages of teams that invested heavily before it arrived, The Race says. He wants a sliding-scale catch-up mechanism that would let teams outside the top four spend more, with the most freedom for those at the bottom of the standings.

The name borrows from ADUO, the Additional Development and Upgrade Opportunities system that already gives power unit makers who are behind the benchmark extra spending, dyno hours and homologation chances. [Read Motorsport](https://readmotorsport.com/2026/11/14/f1-cost-cap-proposal-struggling-teams/) adds that Williams has had to spend heavily on factory equipment and operating systems, leaving less room under the cap for car development.

## Who supports it

According to The Race, only Cadillac, Alpine, Aston Martin and Audi back the idea so far. Haas is against it because it does not spend near the current cap, and Racing Bulls team boss Alan Permane has said he does not support the concept.

## When it could happen

To change the rules for 2027, the plan would need a supermajority: the FIA, Formula One Management and six of the 11 teams. The Race says support from four teams would be enough for it to come into play for 2028, ahead of the next rules era due in 2030 or 2031. Read Motorsport notes that no decision has been announced and discussions are expected to continue after Monday.

Analysis: the top teams have little to gain from a catch-up allowance, but they are pushing their own cost cap changes, including on staff salaries, so Monday's discussion may widen into a broader debate about how the cap works.

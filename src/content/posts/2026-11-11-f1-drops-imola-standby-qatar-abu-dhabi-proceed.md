Formula 1 has told Imola it will not be required to host a late-season race, [Sky Sports reported](https://www.skysports.com/f1/news/13610302/formula-1-proceeding-with-plans-to-finish-2026-season-in-middle-east-as-deadline-to-hold-race-at-imola-passes) on 10 November, after a deadline to move a season-ending round to Italy passed.

According to Sky, Imola had been on standby to stage a race in the first week of December if F1 decided the final two scheduled rounds, in Qatar and Abu Dhabi, could not be held safely. Officials at the Italian circuit were informed on Tuesday that they will not be needed, which Sky said confirms F1's intention to go ahead with those two events as planned.

The remaining calendar, as listed by Sky, is the Las Vegas Grand Prix on 20-22 November, the Qatar Grand Prix on 27-29 November and the Abu Dhabi Grand Prix on 4-6 December. The three races form a season-ending triple-header. Sky noted that F1 had already had to cancel April races in Bahrain and Saudi Arabia because of conflict in the Middle East, and that the Bahrain Grand Prix was later staged in Malaysia, leaving the season one race short of its original 24-round schedule.

F1 president Stefano Domenicali has said throughout the season that the finales would be held at their scheduled venues unless the situation deteriorated, Sky reported, and that the safety of everyone involved remains the priority. Sky added that it is understood F1 would still cancel the races if the situation changed in the coming weeks, and that there has been no suggestion any of the 11 teams opposes completing the season as planned.

Sky also reported that FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem posted on Instagram on Tuesday: "We are ready for Qatar and Abu Dhabi. We are fully prepared and committed to delivering two outstanding Grands Prix weekends and a spectacular conclusion to the FIA Formula One World Championship. We look forward to welcoming drivers, teams and fans to two iconic circuits for an unforgettable finish to the season. See you on the grid."

The Lusail International Circuit in Qatar staged a MotoGP round on 6-8 November. Sky said there had been security concerns, and that Yamaha did not attend, but the event passed without incident. Those points are from Sky's report; they are not an independent security assessment.

A calendar confirmation of this kind is still subject to later changes if organisers decide they cannot proceed. As of Sky's Tuesday report, F1 is proceeding with Qatar and Abu Dhabi after the Imola standby option lapsed.

Formula 1 fans may soon see how hard a driver's heart is working at 300km/h. "We will be able to see heart rate data of F1 drivers on TV broadcasts from 2027 after wearables company WHOOP signed on with the motorsport series," [Autosport posted on X](https://x.com/autosport/status/2123153119307567368) on Wednesday.

## The deal

According to [Autosport's report](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/f1-announces-new-partnership-with-whoop/10875346/), WHOOP is joining F1 as a commercial partner. Drivers who sign up will wear WHOOP bands inside the car, and data such as heart rate will feed F1's storytelling, from social media graphics to overlays on the live TV broadcast, as early as 2027.

F1 did not say how many drivers will take part. Autosport understands deals are being finalised case by case and at least a handful of drivers have already signed up. Ferrari signed its own WHOOP deal earlier this year, and Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc already use the bands.

## What the bands measure

Autosport says the devices track heart rate, heart rate variability, sleep quality and VO2 max. F1's announcement says the partnership will deliver "select insights" in collaboration with the drivers, giving fans "a real-time window into the physical realities of racing".

WHOOP chief executive Will Ahmed said: "For decades, fans have had incredible insight into the cars, but less insight into the impact that races have on the drivers. Now, for the first time, we can show what happens inside a driver's body during the most intense moments of a race."

## Why it is opt-in

The wording matters. F1 talks about insights delivered "in the right way in collaboration with the drivers", and is signing them one by one. Health data is personal, so expect drivers to have a say in what is shown. This weekend's race is covered in our [Las Vegas hub](/hubs/las-vegas/).

## Our read

Analysis: heart rate overlays have worked in other sports because they turn effort into something viewers can see. In F1 the most interesting numbers may come from a start, a safety car restart or a last-lap defence, if drivers agree to share them.

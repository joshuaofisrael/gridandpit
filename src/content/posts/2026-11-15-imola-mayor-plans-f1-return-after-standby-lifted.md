Imola will not host a Formula 1 race this year, but its mayor wants it back on the calendar. Marco Panieri said on Instagram that the city's push to bring the "Made in Italy" and Emilia-Romagna Grand Prix back "continues", [PlanetF1 reports](https://www.planetf1.com/news/imola-reveals-future-f1-return-plan).

## Why Imola was on standby

F1 had lined up Imola as a fall-back finale on 6 December in case the Qatar (29 November) and Abu Dhabi (6 December) races could not go ahead because of the conflict in the Middle East, [The Race explains](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/f1-stands-imola-down-what-it-means-for-2026-and-27-calendars/). F1 was willing to underwrite the estimated $10-15 million cost of the extra race. This week it told Imola it would not be needed, as it was happy to continue with the original plan, while keeping the Middle East races under constant review.

PlanetF1 adds that if those races are cancelled, no replacement will be sought and the Las Vegas Grand Prix would become the season finale.

## What Panieri said

Panieri wrote that the city would have loved to share the moment with new world champion Kimi Antonelli, the teams and fans. He said Imola would keep working with Emilia-Romagna president Michele de Pascale, the region, ACI and other partners to promote the area's Motor Valley and show it is ready to host F1.

Imola's previous contract expired after 2025. The circuit hosted the San Marino Grand Prix from 1981 to 2006 and returned in 2020, PlanetF1 notes, though the 2023 race was cancelled because of flooding. It is not yet clear whether Imola wants an annual slot or a rotating one.

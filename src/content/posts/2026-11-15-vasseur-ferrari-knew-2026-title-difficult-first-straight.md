Ferrari team principal Fred Vasseur says the team realised at the very start of the season that winning the 2026 title would be difficult. "We understood from the first straight line," he said, [according to PlanetF1](https://www.planetf1.com/news/ferrari-f1-2026-mercedes-title-loss-concession), pointing to a straight-line speed deficit that two engine upgrades have not fully closed.

## Six or seven tenths

Vasseur estimated that Mercedes were six to seven tenths of a second faster than Ferrari over the first six races. He said the team had done a good job developing the engine since then and that on the chassis side it was "more than performant".

Mercedes clinched the constructors' championship in Brazil last weekend. PlanetF1 notes that Ferrari is likely to finish second, holding a 95-point lead over Red Bull with three races left, which would extend a run without a title that now stretches to 18 years.

## Better since Malaysia

Vasseur said Ferrari has been much more competitive since Malaysia, where Lewis Hamilton's third place ended a five-race run without a podium, while admitting a couple of missed opportunities, including the first-lap clash between his drivers in Mexico. He suggested Ferrari may have scored more points than anyone since the Madrid race.

Brazil was a setback. Charles Leclerc and Hamilton finished eighth and ninth after grid penalties, and Vasseur told [Formula1.com](F1V) that the weekend was largely about getting the tyres into the right temperature window, combined with the team's top-speed problem.

Analysis: a car that is quick in corners but slow on the straights is hard to race from the back of the grid. Fixing that power deficit over the winter is the obvious priority if Ferrari is to challenge in 2027.

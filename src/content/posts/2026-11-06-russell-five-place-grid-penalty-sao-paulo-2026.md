George Russell will take a five-place grid penalty at this weekend's Sao Paulo Grand Prix for use of a fresh Mercedes engine.

In a [post on 6 November 2026](https://x.com/SkySportsF1/status/2118709328190619746), verified Sky Sports F1 wrote: "George Russell will take a five-place grid penalty at this weekend's Sao Paulo Grand Prix for use of a fresh Mercedes engine 🇧🇷"

## Why five places, and why Interlagos

[Sky Sports](https://www.skysports.com/f1/news/13608847/george-russell-mercedes-driver-to-take-five-place-grid-penalty-at-sao-paulo-gp-for-engine-change) reports that Mercedes is fitting a sixth internal combustion engine of the season at Interlagos. Russell already took a fifth engine and other power-unit parts at the Singapore Grand Prix, which dropped him to the back of that grid after a Malaysia (Bahrain GP) power-unit failure. Because the ICE allocation had already been exceeded, Sky Sports says this latest change triggers a five-place demotion rather than a full back-of-grid hit.

Sky Sports also says Mercedes confirmed there is no Additional Development and Upgrade Opportunities (ADUO) element in this change, with the team still to introduce its one allowed upgrade this season.

## Russell's own framing

[Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/well-try-and-come-back-through-russell-set-for-grid-penalty-at-sao-paulo-grand-prix.6hsUcdvDTFFezGL53SAODZ) and Sky Sports both quote Russell from a Mercedes Instagram Stories video. He said the team always had to take another grid penalty somewhere after Singapore, felt Interlagos was a good place to take it, recalled Lewis Hamilton recovering from a big Brazil grid penalty years ago, and added that five places "doesn't seem that bad" and "we'll try and come back through."

For Grid and Pit readers, the weekend takeaway is a sourced five-place drop for Russell's fresh ICE at Sao Paulo, with Mercedes saying the change is not an ADUO upgrade and Russell arguing Interlagos is a workable track to serve it.

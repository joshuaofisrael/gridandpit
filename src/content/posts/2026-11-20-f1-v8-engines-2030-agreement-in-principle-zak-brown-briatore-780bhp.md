The V8s are coming back, if everyone keeps their word. "Zak Brown confirms that there is an 'agreement in principle' for V8 engines to return to F1 in the future," [Sky Sports F1 posted on X](https://x.com/SkySportsF1/status/2123699342553043041) on Friday morning.

## What Brown said

The McLaren CEO spoke at Thursday's team representatives' press conference in Las Vegas. "There's an agreement in principle. I think everyone is very, very excited," he said, according to [Motorsport.com](MSV8). "I think now it's about kind of dotting the I's and crossing the T's."

That cleared up a confusing week. Motorsport.com says official FIA social channels briefly published and then deleted graphics announcing the deal. Alpine's Flavio Briatore went further, as quoted by [BBC Sport](BBCV8): "We have the agreement, basically. It's not signed, but it looks like everybody's agreed to 2030 V8s, and I'm very happy."

## The engine

BBC Sport reports the rules are for a 2.1-litre V8 turbo with a smaller electric part than this year: a maximum of 200kW compared with 350kW now. Only half of that will be available all the time, with the rest kept for an overtake mode. With about 780bhp from the combustion engine, BBC Sport puts total output at about 1,050bhp in overtake mode and just over 900bhp in normal running.

The aim is to end the energy starvation drivers have criticised this year. BBC Sport adds that cars should start with about 110-120kg of fuel, power units will have a 150kg weight limit, and the engine budget cap will be $190m a year. GM, which backs Cadillac, will delay its own engine to 2030.

## Don't expect the old scream

BBC Sport says the engines are expected to sound similar to today's, because a big jump in noise could threaten street races such as Miami and Las Vegas. FIA President Mohammed Ben Sulayem had first pushed for a naturally aspirated V8 with no hybrid.

## What happens next

Motorsport.com says the framework is due to go to the FIA World Motor Sport Council in Shanghai next month. A 2030 start means cutting the current engine cycle short by a year. Engine supply deals, such as the one in our [Red Bull-Ford story](/articles/2026-10-28-red-bull-ford-engine-supply-mekies/), will be planned around that date.

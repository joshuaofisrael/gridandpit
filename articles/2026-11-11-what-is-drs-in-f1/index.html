What Happened to DRS? F1's 2026 Active Aero and Overtake Mode Explained

By Grid and Pit

DRS is gone from Formula 1 in 2026. Here's how Straight Mode active aero and the new Overtake Mode work, how the one-second detection rule carried over, and why Monaco runs without active aero.

Last updated November 11, 2026

For more than a decade, the Drag Reduction System (DRS) was Formula 1's overtaking aid. A driver within one second of the car ahead could open a flap in the rear wing on certain straights. That shed drag and gained top speed. In 2026, with new chassis and power unit rules, DRS in that form is gone. Two new systems replaced it: active aerodynamics (Straight Mode and Corner Mode), which every car uses, and Overtake Mode, an electrical power boost for the chasing car. Here is how both work, based on the FIA's 2026 Sporting Regulations and Formula 1's own explainers.

What DRS did, in brief

DRS let a following driver open the rear wing's upper flap in designated zones, but only if they were within one second of the car in front at a detection point. Formula 1 notes that the DRS rear wing dates from 2011. It was designed to make passing easier on long straights.

Active aero: Straight Mode and Corner Mode

From 2026 the cars have full-time active aerodynamics. The FIA's regulations call it "Driver Adjustable Bodywork". It lets the angle of both the front wing and the rear wing flap change, under the control of the FIA's standard ECU.

  • Corner Mode is the default "closed" position. The wings make maximum downforce for grip in the corners.
  • Straight Mode is the "open", low-drag position. The rear wing flap opens a gap, as DRS did, and the uppermost front wing elements drop at the same time. That flattens the wings, cuts drag and raises top speed.

The key difference from DRS is that anyone can use it, on every lap. Formula 1 explains that the rear wing can open on defined straights "without the need to be within one second of the car in front." It is not an overtaking aid as such. It is an efficiency setting that every car uses wherever the FIA allows it.

Where and when it can be used

  • Activation zones. The FIA tells teams the activation zones for each circuit at least four weeks before the event. Each zone start is marked by trackside signage. Fans now see boards marked "SM" (Straight Mode) where the DRS boards used to be. Formula 1 reported four such zones in Bahrain pre-season testing.
  • Driver control. Drivers open the wings manually for each zone, and only once the car's control electronics tell them it is enabled. The wings close if the driver brakes or lifts off the throttle, or closes them manually. New for 2026, some zones have a lap-distance cut-off so drivers can't take certain corners with the wings still open.
  • Low-grip conditions. When race control declares low grip, for example in the wet, only partial activation is allowed. The front wing goes to its low-drag position but the rear flap stays closed, and in shorter "low grip" zones.
  • Zone rules. Formula 1 reports that each zone must last more than three seconds. Zones are designed with tyre grip margins in mind, and some have been shortened or removed to limit speeds into the following corner.

Why Monaco ran without it

At Monaco the FIA chose not to use active aero at all. The cars were effectively locked in Corner Mode all weekend because the tight street circuit has so few straights. Overtake Mode still applied.

Overtake Mode: the new "within one second" tool

The idea of rewarding the chasing car survives in Overtake Mode, which works through the power unit rather than the wings.

How it works under the 2026 Sporting Regulations:

  1. Detection. Each circuit has a Detection Line, marked by a solid yellow line across the track, and an Activation Line. The FIA sets their positions and the "Detection Gap". Formula 1 describes the gap as one second, with the detection point usually out of the final corner.
  2. Activation. If a driver crosses the Detection Line less than the Detection Gap behind another car, Overtake is activated at the Activation Line and can be used on the following lap.
  3. The benefit. Formula 1 says Overtake Mode lets the driver recharge an extra 0.5MJ of electrical energy. It also unlocks an additional electrical power profile, so the car can "sustain a higher speed for a longer period." This is most useful on long straights.
  4. When it is switched off. Overtake is disabled at the start or restart of a race until the leader first crosses the Detection Line. It is disabled while the Safety Car is out, and in low-grip conditions. The Race Director can also disable it for safety.

In qualifying and practice, which the rules call lap-time classified sessions, Overtake is simply enabled and available all the time.

Boost: the driver's own button

Separate from Overtake Mode, drivers have a Boost button. Formula 1 explains that pressing it deploys stored electrical energy at any point, either at maximum power or in a profile configured by the team. Unlike Overtake Mode, it isn't tied to the gap to the car ahead, so drivers can use it to attack or defend. The trade-off is energy: whatever they spend has to be harvested again.

What changes for fans watching

  • No more DRS "trains" in the old sense. Every car opens its wings on the straights, so the car ahead no longer has a built-in drag disadvantage.
  • Passing depends on energy management. Who saved charge, who has Overtake Mode, and who spends Boost where are the new strategic questions. Overtakes may come in places they used to be rare.
  • Trackside markers changed. Look for SM boards at activation zones and the yellow detection line before the final sector.

The short version

DRS is gone in 2026. Every car now opens its front and rear wing elements on designated straights in Straight Mode, with no one-second rule. The one-second rule lives on in Overtake Mode: be within the detection gap at the detection line and you get extra electrical energy and power on the next lap. Low grip, Safety Cars and race control can switch the systems off, and at Monaco active aero was not used at all.

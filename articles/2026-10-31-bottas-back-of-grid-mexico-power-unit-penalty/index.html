Bottas will start the Mexico City Grand Prix from the back after five new power unit parts

By Grid and Pit

Cadillac fitted Valtteri Bottas with five new power unit elements beyond the season allocation, adding up to a 50 place grid penalty that sends him to the back of the grid on Sunday.

Last updated October 31, 2026

Valtteri Bottas will start Sunday's Mexico City Grand Prix at the back of the grid. Cadillac fitted his car with five new power unit elements beyond the number allowed for the season, and the penalties add up to 50 places, according to Formula 1 and GrandPrix.com.

Which parts were changed

The FIA notice listed on the Mexico City decision page covers five elements. Bottas is now on his fifth internal combustion engine and fifth turbocharger, and his fourth energy store, fourth set of control electronics and fourth MGU K, the motor generator unit that recovers energy under braking.

Each part was the first time this season he went past the limit for that element, and a first breach costs 10 grid places. Five elements at 10 places each makes 50.

Why it means the back, not a pit lane start

Under the sporting rules, once a driver's combined drop goes past 15 places it is converted into a start from the back of the grid instead of the full number, as PlanetF1 explains. So the size of the total, 50 places, matters less than the fact that it is over that threshold.

PlanetF1 notes that Cadillac, which runs Ferrari power units in its first Formula 1 season, has mostly been fighting at the back in recent rounds, so the practical cost may be limited. The team is still looking for its first championship point, with Bottas's 13th in China its best result so far.

Bottas's Friday

Bottas sat out first practice while Colton Herta drove his car, then was 21st of 22 in second practice. In the Cadillac notes published by Formula 1, he said the team still had to find the right setup and that "we also need to keep an eye on reliability, particularly with the brakes and engine."

Team principal Marcin Budkowski said Mexico City is a difficult circuit for cooling and brakes, but that the team had a good baseline to improve overnight.

The bigger Cadillac picture

The penalty lands while Cadillac weighs its 2027 lineup. PlanetF1 reports that the team has given Sergio Pérez a new multi year contract and that Bottas is understood to remain the preference for the second seat, while the team assesses its options, including test driver Colton Herta. Pérez told reporters it is "not my decision" and that "it's not an easy decision for the team."

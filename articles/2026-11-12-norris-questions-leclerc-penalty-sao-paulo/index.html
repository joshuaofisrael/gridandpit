Norris questions Leclerc's five-second Sao Paulo penalty for contact with Lawson

By Grid and Pit

Race winner Lando Norris said on a Twitch stream that Charles Leclerc's late contact with Liam Lawson at Interlagos was 'just racing', disagreeing with the stewards who dropped Leclerc to eighth.

Last updated November 12, 2026

Lando Norris has said he disagrees with the five-second penalty that cost Charles Leclerc seventh place at the Sao Paulo Grand Prix, Crash.net reported on Wednesday.

Norris, who won the wet race for McLaren, made the comments during a Twitch stream with his friend and former racer Max Fewtrell. According to Crash.net, Norris asked how Leclerc got the penalty and described the contact as part of normal racing. He ended the exchange by saying: "I thought it was a perfectly placed nudge." Read Motorsport carried the same account on Thursday.

What the stewards decided

Leclerc touched the left rear of Lawson's Racing Bulls at Turn 12 and passed him five corners later, on the penultimate lap, Formula1.com reported on race night. The stewards said the contact was minor but ruled that it disrupted Lawson and let Leclerc close in and complete the pass, which gave Leclerc a clear sporting advantage. They added that under the driving standard guidelines Lawson had the right to the corner.

The stewards cited the wet conditions and the minor nature of the contact as reasons for a five-second penalty rather than 10 seconds, per Formula1.com. Lawson crossed the line just under two seconds behind, so the two swapped places and Leclerc was classified eighth.

The same stewards also gave five-second penalties to Kimi Antonelli and Ollie Bearman for unsafe releases in the pit lane. Neither lost a position.

Why it matters

Analysis: Norris's view does not change the result, and drivers often disagree with stewards about contact in wet conditions. It does add a race winner's voice to a debate over where hard racing ends, and the swap moved Lawson up to seventh as Racing Bulls fight to hold fifth in the constructors' standings. Racing Bulls kept Lawson for 2027 on Thursday.

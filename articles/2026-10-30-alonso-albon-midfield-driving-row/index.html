Albon, Perez and Leclerc push back after Alonso criticizes driving at the back of the grid

By Grid and Pit

Fernando Alonso stood by his criticism of the standard of driving in the lower third of the grid after his Austin battle with Alex Albon. Albon, Sergio Perez, Charles Leclerc, Pierre Gasly and Oliver Bearman all disagreed in Mexico City.

Last updated October 30, 2026

Fernando Alonso's view that the standard of driving is low in the lower third of the grid became the talking point of Thursday's media day in Mexico City, and the drivers asked about it disagreed with him.

How it started

Alonso and Williams driver Alex Albon fought for most of the race in Austin last Sunday. Afterward Alonso was critical of Albon and of the level of driving at the back. On Thursday, Sky Sports reporter Craig Slater told the FIA press conference that Alonso had stood by those remarks, calling it "a very low level of driving" and saying: "I still find it difficult to understand what was the opportunity that Alex saw in the moment." Alonso was not part of the FIA sessions, so those words come from Slater's question.

Albon: respect, but no agreement

Albon said he has looked up to Alonso since he was a child. "I think he's a great driver," he said. He called their Austin fight "a good scrap" and said he saw the incident as roughly even, given where the cars were on track.

On the wider point he was firm. "If anything, I would say the grid's the strongest it's been for a while," Albon said. Pressed again, he added: "It's not nice to hear those comments in many ways... but at the same time, I don't agree. It's as simple as that." He said the back half of the grid is stronger than it was ten or fifteen years ago because "you don't have pay drivers anymore," and finished with: "Hopefully, we don't cross paths in the race this weekend."

Perez and Leclerc back Albon

Cadillac's Sergio Perez, whose team is last in the constructors' table, said results mostly reflect machinery. "If we swapped cars with the guys at the front, we'd have very similar results, or vice versa," he said. "You are as good as your car and your team."

Ferrari's Charles Leclerc called the level "very, very high" from front to back, noting that most of the field grew up racing each other in karts. He said Albon was a driver he looked up to back then, always "one or two categories above me."

Gasly and Bearman

In the earlier session, Alpine's Pierre Gasly joked that he was pleased to be in the first half of the grid, said he had not seen the race in question, and called the current field one of the strongest he has seen in about nine years. Haas driver Oliver Bearman called Alonso's conclusion "slightly harsh." He said that with the top four teams filling the top eight places, midfield drivers have to be opportunistic, "and that can end in disaster."

Why it matters

Alonso and Albon race in the same part of the field, so another close fight is possible this weekend. Mexico City's long run to Turn 1 often produces close racing on the opening lap.

How the rookies fared in Mexico City FP1: Vesti third, Crawford stopped early and three debuts

By Grid and Pit

Ten of the 11 teams ran a young driver in first practice in Mexico City. Frederik Vesti was third for Mercedes, American Jak Crawford stopped with a battery problem, and Nikola Tsolov, Gabriele Mini and Freddie Slater made their Formula 1 session debuts.

Last updated October 31, 2026

First practice at the Mexico City Grand Prix was the busiest rookie session of the year. Ten of the 11 teams put a young driver in one of their cars, and only Ferrari ran both of its race drivers, according to the FIA and Formula 1. Charles Leclerc topped the session. We set out who was driving where in our FP1 rookie preview.

Where each young driver finished

Times are from the drivers' entries in the team notes published by Formula 1:

  1. Frederik Vesti, Mercedes (for George Russell): 3rd, 1:18.861
  2. Ayumu Iwasa, Red Bull (for Max Verstappen): 5th, 1:19.113
  3. Nikola Tsolov, Racing Bulls (for Liam Lawson): 12th, 1:19.604, first Formula 1 session
  4. Gabriele Mini, Alpine (for Franco Colapinto): 14th, 1:19.946, first Formula 1 session
  5. Ryo Hirakawa, Haas (for Esteban Ocon): 15th, 1:19.982
  6. Leonardo Fornaroli, McLaren (for Lando Norris): 17th, 1:20.235
  7. Jak Crawford, Aston Martin (for Lance Stroll): 19th, 1:20.803
  8. Luke Browning, Williams (for Alex Albon): 20th, 1:20.869
  9. Freddie Slater, Audi (for Gabriel Bortoleto): 21st, 1:20.945, first Formula 1 session
  10. Colton Herta, Cadillac (for Valtteri Bottas): 22nd, 1:21.365

Vesti the standout

Vesti was third in Russell's car, behind only Leclerc and Kimi Antonelli. He said seeing third on the screens made him "extremely happy" and noted he had worked on the car since the start of its development two years ago. Mercedes engineer Andrew Shovlin said he "did an excellent job."

The two Americans

Crawford's session ended early. Aston Martin told him to stop on track, and he said in the team notes that it was "due to a battery issue." The FIA report says his stop brought out a virtual safety car. Team ambassador Pedro de la Rosa said the problem cut his session short "slightly."

Herta completed 30 laps in Bottas's Cadillac, according to PlanetF1, and finished last on time. The run counts toward his super license, but a correction to the 2025 IndyCar standings means he is still one point short, as we explain in Herta is still one point short of a super license.

Three debuts

Tsolov finished two places behind his Racing Bulls teammate Arvid Lindblad. "It was a super special day today and something I've dreamt of since I started racing," he said. The BBC notes that he is one of three drivers in contention for two Racing Bulls seats next year, which ties into the Verstappen and Lawson clash later in the day.

Mini said it was "an amazing experience" and described waiting in the pit lane behind Lewis Hamilton. Slater's first run was cut short after Audi released him with a cooling fan still attached to the car, and he had to return to the pits. The stewards fined Audi 7,500 euros for the unsafe release, according to the FIA decision page. Slater said he "cannot wait for Brazil now."

What comes next

Fornaroli and Browning both said they are due back in FP1 at Interlagos next weekend. US times for that event are in our São Paulo Grand Prix guide, and the full Friday picture is in our practice report. For the wider seat picture, see the 2027 driver market tracker.

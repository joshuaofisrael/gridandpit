F1 tiebreak rules explained: why ninth place is enough for Antonelli in Mexico City

By Grid and Pit

When two drivers finish level on points, the title goes to the one with more wins. That countback rule is why Antonelli can clinch with ninth, and why tenth works unless Russell wins.

Last updated October 27, 2026

Most coverage of Kimi Antonelli's title chance in Mexico City says he needs ninth place. F1 Oversteer goes a step further and says tenth is enough unless George Russell wins. Both are right, and the reason is a rule that rarely gets attention: what happens when two drivers end the season level on points.

How points are awarded in 2026

A Grand Prix pays 25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2 and 1 point to the top ten finishers. There is no bonus point for the fastest lap. Sprint races pay 8 points down to 1 for the top eight, but all six Sprints on the 2026 calendar are already done. That scoring is set out by Rules Delta, which cites the FIA sporting regulations, and matches the scoring table on Wikipedia's 2026 season page.

So after Mexico City, the only points left are from four Grands Prix in Brazil, Las Vegas, Qatar and Abu Dhabi. That is a maximum of 100 points per driver, as Sky Sports also points out.

The countback rule

If two drivers finish level on points, the one with more race wins ranks higher. If wins are equal, the count moves to second places, then thirds, and so on until there is a difference. The official beginner's guide explains it the same way. Rules Delta adds that the 2026 rules include a further step that applies the same countback to qualifying results if drivers are still level, before the FIA has to make a call.

Applying it to Antonelli

The official standings after Austin have Antonelli on 365 points, Russell on 242 and Lewis Hamilton on 241. Antonelli has nine Grand Prix wins this year. Russell has three and Hamilton has one, according to the race results listed on Wikipedia.

Now take the worst case for Antonelli in each scenario, where his rival wins every remaining race he can and Antonelli scores nothing after Mexico City. These are Grid and Pit's own calculations from those published totals.

Antonelli finishes in Mexico Rival scenario Rival's final total Antonelli's final total Who is champion
Ninth (2 points) Russell 367 367 Antonelli, 9 wins to 8
Tenth (1 point) Hamilton 366 366 Antonelli, 9 wins to 6
Tenth (1 point) Russell 367 366 Russell
Outside the points Neither, because someone else wins in Mexico 360 at most 365 Antonelli

The first two rows are the key. In both cases the rival would draw level on points, but even five straight wins would leave Russell on eight victories and Hamilton on six. Antonelli's nine wins settle the tie in his favor. That is why ninth place is always enough, and why tenth is enough unless the Mexico City winner is Russell, whose total would then edge one point past.

If neither Russell nor Hamilton wins on Sunday, the most either could reach is far short of Antonelli's total, so he is champion wherever he finishes. Our Mexico City scenarios piece covers that side of the math.

A tie that already matters

Countback is not just theory this year. Red Bull and McLaren are level on 355 points in the teams' standings, with Red Bull listed third. Each team has two wins. By Grid and Pit's count of the race results on Wikipedia, Red Bull has four second places to McLaren's two, which is what puts Max Verstappen's team ahead for now. With four races left and no Sprints, that battle for third could swing on a single podium. The full table is on our points page and in our standings after Austin.

The Mexico City Grand Prix starts Sunday, November 1 at 3 PM Eastern. Every session in your zone is in our race times guide, and our weekend preview runs through the storylines.

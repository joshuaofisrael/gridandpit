Youngest F1 world champions: Antonelli is first at 20 years and 68 days

By Grid and Pit

Kimi Antonelli won the 2026 title at 20 years and 68 days, ahead of Sebastian Vettel at 23 years and 134 days.

Last updated November 20, 2026

Update, 20 November 2026: Antonelli is the champion. He finished third in Mexico City on 1 November 2026. Guinness World Records, checked 20 November 2026, lists him at 20 years and 68 days, born 25 August 2006. The Wikipedia list of champions, checked the same day, puts him first on the youngest list. Our Mexico City result has the classification.

The page below was written while the title was still open. The ages in the table are the current youngest ten, not a forecast.

How old he was

Antonelli was born on 25 August 2006, which both Guinness and his Mercedes profile give, the profile placing the birth in Bologna. From 25 August to 1 November is 68 days. That is the same figure Guinness publishes for the record.

He was more than three years younger than Sebastian Vettel, who had held the mark at 23 years and 134 days.

The youngest champions

These are the youngest drivers to win a first title. Ages are from the Wikipedia youngest champions table, checked 20 November 2026. Where a driver won more than once, only the first title is counted, which is how that table is built.

Rank Driver Age at first title Season
1 Kimi Antonelli 20 years, 68 days 2026
2 Sebastian Vettel 23 years, 134 days 2010
3 Lewis Hamilton 23 years, 300 days 2008
4 Fernando Alonso 24 years, 58 days 2005
5 Max Verstappen 24 years, 73 days 2021
6 Emerson Fittipaldi 25 years, 273 days 1972
7 Michael Schumacher 25 years, 314 days 1994
8 Lando Norris 26 years, 24 days 2025
9 Niki Lauda 26 years, 197 days 1975
10 Jacques Villeneuve 26 years, 200 days 1997

Guinness matches Antonelli's line: 20 years and 68 days, at the Mexico City Grand Prix. Vettel's 23 years and 134 days is the same figure Guinness used for the previous record, and it is still the second line on the Wikipedia table.

Jim Clark, at 27 years and 188 days in 1963, was tenth on this list before Antonelli won. He is no longer inside the ten. This check did not reopen a source for his new rank, so the page does not assign him one.

Italy's wait is over

Before the race, Reuters said a title would make Antonelli Italy's first champion in 73 years. The last before him was Alberto Ascari, who won in 1952 and 1953, which Wikipedia's champions list still shows, along with Giuseppe Farina in 1950. The same list now includes Antonelli for 2026. Reuters also noted nine wins that season before Mexico City, including Monza, where he became the first Italian winner there since 1966. Guinness, checked 20 November 2026, records nine wins at the moment he became champion.

"It would be very cool to close this off, to be the next Italian champion, but of course then this has to be just the beginning," Antonelli told Reuters in Austin, before the race.

The American comparison

Only two Americans have won the drivers' title, both on the Wikipedia list: Phil Hill in 1961 and Mario Andretti in 1978. STATS F1 listed their ages as 34 and 38 when this page was first checked on 28 October 2026. Those two ages were not opened again on 20 November. Antonelli's 20 years and 68 days, from Guinness that day, is far younger than either figure.

What the Mexico math was

Before the race he led Russell by 123 points, in our standings after Austin. Ninth would have been enough, and tenth would have been enough unless Russell won. That fork is in the tiebreak explainer and in how he clinched. He finished third. The points system is the scale those sums used.

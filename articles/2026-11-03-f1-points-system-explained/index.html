F1 points system explained: how drivers and teams score

By Grid and Pit

Formula 1 pays points to the top 10 in a Grand Prix, from 25 for a win to 1 for tenth, and to the top 8 in a Sprint. There has been no bonus point for fastest lap since 2025. Both drivers' points count toward the team title.

Last updated November 3, 2026

Formula 1 awards points to the first 10 finishers in every Grand Prix and the first 8 in every Sprint. Those points decide two titles at once: the drivers' championship and the constructors' championship. The rules sit in the FIA's Formula 1 Sporting Regulations, and the full history of past scales is collected in a Wikipedia list.

Grand Prix points

A full distance Grand Prix pays the top 10 like this:

  • 1st: 25 points
  • 2nd: 18
  • 3rd: 15
  • 4th: 12
  • 5th: 10
  • 6th: 8
  • 7th: 6
  • 8th: 4
  • 9th: 2
  • 10th: 1

Eleventh place and lower score nothing. A driver must be classified to score, which in practice means completing enough of the race distance.

Sprint points

Sprint weekends add a shorter Saturday race of about 100 km. It pays the top 8 on a sliding scale: 8 points for the win, then 7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 for eighth. A Sprint can swing a close title fight, because a strong Sprint is worth almost as much as a fourth place finish on Sunday.

No more fastest lap point

From 2019 to 2024, a driver who set the fastest lap and finished in the top 10 picked up one extra point. That bonus was dropped from the 2025 season, so the scale above is the whole story.

Shortened races

When a race is stopped early, usually for rain, points depend on how much distance was completed. The regulations set reduced scales for races that end before 75% distance, and no points are given if fewer than two laps were run under green flag conditions. The rule was tightened after the 2021 Belgian Grand Prix, where half points were awarded after laps behind the Safety Car.

The constructors' championship

Each team runs two cars, and both drivers' points add up for the team. That is why a quiet ninth place for a team's second driver can matter as much as a podium in the constructors' fight, where prize money depends on final position.

Ties

If two drivers finish level on points, the title goes to the one with more wins, then more second places, and so on down the order. We walked through that in our tiebreak explainer.

Quick math for fans

A driver who wins every Grand Prix in a 24 race season would score 600 points before Sprints. In practice, titles are usually decided by consistency: a run of second places at 18 points each can beat a rival who mixes wins with retirements.

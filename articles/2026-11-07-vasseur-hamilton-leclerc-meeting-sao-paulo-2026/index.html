Vasseur says Ferrari meeting with Hamilton and Leclerc after Mexico clash went well

By Grid and Pit

Ferrari team principal Frédéric Vasseur said a Thursday-evening meeting at Interlagos with Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc after their Mexico lap-1 Turn 4 clash went well, with guidelines unchanged and a clear message to put the team first.

Last updated November 7, 2026

Ferrari team principal Frédéric Vasseur said a Thursday-evening meeting at Interlagos with Lewis Hamilton and Charles Leclerc after their Mexico City lap-one clash went well, with team-first guidelines unchanged ahead of the Sao Paulo Grand Prix.

Sky Sports reports that the three spoke on Thursday evening at Interlagos, four days after the team-mates' first-lap Turn 4 incident in Mexico that opened the door for Max Verstappen to win. Vasseur spoke in Friday's team principals' press conference.

What Vasseur said (as reported)

According to Sky Sports, Vasseur said: "We did it [talked] yesterday evening because I had to fly back to Europe between the two races, but it went well." He added that it was good to discuss and clarify positions, then: "Now we turn the page and we are focused on the next one."

On guidelines, Sky Sports quotes him saying they remain the same: the drivers need to respect each other, and "mainly and firstly" respect the brand, the team, people working at home and people working on track. "I think we are putting a lot of effort on this, and the message was clear and will stay clear."

Sky Sports also quotes Vasseur saying Ferrari "failed collectively" when a potential one-two became a non-win in Mexico, and that the meeting was a clear reminder that Ferrari comes first for the brand, employees and tifosi. He did not announce a new driver hierarchy.

BBC Sport similarly reports that talks happened Thursday evening after Leclerc and Vasseur returned from Europe between races, and quotes Vasseur on turning the page, unchanged respect guidelines, and Ferrari coming first.

RTBF attribution on "too far"

Separately, RTBF reported Vasseur telling Gaëtan Vigneron that both drivers recognised they had gone too far, that the result and the comments were not acceptable, and that the team needed to turn the page. That wording is attributed to RTBF's report of Vasseur's remarks to its reporter, not to the Friday press-conference quotes carried by Sky Sports and BBC.

Grid and Pit takeaway

For readers, the sourced picture is: a Thursday Interlagos meeting happened; Vasseur says it went well; guidelines on mutual and team respect are unchanged; he wants the page turned with Ferrari first; RTBF separately attributes a "too far" acknowledgement to both drivers. No outlet cited here reports a new number-one / number-two order.
